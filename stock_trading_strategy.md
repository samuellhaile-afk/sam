# Stock Trading Strategy Guide (Simple English)

## What Is a Stock Trading Strategy?

A stock trading strategy is a **plan** that tells you:
- **When to buy** a stock
- **When to sell** a stock
- **How much money** to use
- **How to protect** your money if things go wrong

Think of it like a recipe. Instead of guessing, you follow clear rules every time.

---

## Core Principles (Rules to Always Follow)

### 1. Never Risk More Than You Can Afford to Lose
- Only invest money you **do not need** for daily life.
- A good rule: never risk more than **1-2% of your total money** on a single trade.
- Example: If you have $10,000, risk at most $100-$200 on one trade.

### 2. Always Have a Stop-Loss
- A **stop-loss** is a price where you automatically sell to prevent bigger losses.
- Set it before you buy — not after.
- Example: You buy a stock at $50. Set a stop-loss at $45. If it drops to $45, you sell. You lose $5, not $20.

### 3. Know Your Target Price
- Before buying, decide: "At what price will I sell to take my profit?"
- Example: You buy at $50. Your target is $60. When it hits $60, you sell and take your profit.

### 4. Follow the Trend
- Stocks that are going **up tend to keep going up** (for a while).
- Stocks that are going **down tend to keep going down** (for a while).
- Try to trade **with** the trend, not against it.

## A Simple Strategy: The "Buy the Dip" Strategy

This is one of the easiest strategies for beginners.

### What It Means
- A good stock sometimes drops a little in price even when the overall trend is up.
- That small drop is called a **"dip"**.
- You **buy during the dip** and sell when the price goes back up.

### Step-by-Step

**Step 1: Pick a strong stock**
- Look for a company that is well-known and has been growing over the last 6-12 months.
- Examples: large technology, healthcare, or consumer companies.

**Step 2: Watch for a dip**
- The stock should drop **5-10% from its recent high** while the overall market is still healthy.
- This means the dip is temporary, not a sign of serious trouble.

**Step 3: Check that the trend is still up**
- Look at the stock chart. Is the overall direction still pointing up?
- If yes — this is a buying opportunity.
- If no (the stock has been falling for months) — skip it.

**Step 4: Buy the stock**
- Buy a small amount — do not go all in.
- Use only the money you decided to risk for this trade.

**Step 5: Set your stop-loss**
- Place your stop-loss about **2-3% below** the price you paid.
- This limits how much you can lose.

**Step 6: Set your target price**
- Aim for a **10-15% gain** from your buy price.
- When the stock hits that price, sell and take your profit.

**Step 7: Stick to the plan**
- Do not change your stop-loss or target in the middle of the trade out of emotion.
- If the stop-loss triggers — accept the small loss and move on.

## A Simple Strategy: Moving Average Crossover

This strategy uses **moving averages** — a line that shows the average price of a stock over a period of time.

### Two Lines You Need
- **Short moving average (SMA 20)**: Average price over the last 20 days.
- **Long moving average (SMA 50)**: Average price over the last 50 days.

### The Rule
| Signal | What Happens | Action |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **Buy Signal** | Short line crosses **above** long line | Buy the stock |
| **Sell Signal** | Short line crosses **below** long line | Sell the stock |

### Why It Works
- When the recent average price rises above the longer average, it means the stock is picking up momentum — a good time to buy.
- When the recent average drops below the longer average, the stock is losing momentum — time to sell.

## Risk Management (Protecting Your Money)

This is the most important part of any strategy.

| Rule | What It Means |
|------|--------------|
| **1% Rule** | Never risk more than 1% of your total account on one trade |
| **Stop-Loss** | Always set a price where you will automatically sell if the trade goes wrong |
| **Diversify** | Do not put all your money in one stock — spread it across 5-10 stocks |
| **No Revenge Trading** | If you lose, do not immediately make a bigger trade to "win it back" |
| **Keep Records** | Write down every trade: why you bought, when you sold, how much you made or lost |

---

## When NOT to Trade

Avoid trading when:
- There is major news (elections, earnings reports) that could cause big swings.
- You are emotional — angry, excited, or panicked.
- The overall market is in a strong downtrend.
- You do not understand why you are making the trade.

---

## Simple Daily Routine for a Trader

1. **Morning (before market opens):** Check the news. Are there any big events today?
2. **Market open:** Watch your stocks for the first 30 minutes but do not trade immediately — prices are often chaotic at open.
3. **Mid-day:** Look for your setups. Is any stock giving a buy signal?
4. **Before market close:** Review your open positions. Are stop-losses still in the right place?
5. **After market:** Write down what you did and why. Review if it worked.

## Key Terms Glossary

| Term | Simple Meaning |
|------|---------------|
| **Stock** | A small piece of ownership in a company |
| **Buy** | Purchase a stock hoping it will go up |
| **Sell** | Give away your stock, ideally at a higher price than you paid |
| **Stop-Loss** | A price that automatically triggers a sell to limit your loss |
| **Target Price** | The price at which you plan to sell for a profit |
| **Trend** | The overall direction a stock is moving (up or down) |
| **Dip** | A small, temporary price drop during an overall upward trend |
| **Moving Average** | The average price of a stock over a set number of days |
| **Portfolio** | All the stocks you own together |
| **Diversify** | Spread your money across many different stocks to reduce risk |

---

*Remember: No strategy wins 100% of the time. The goal is to win more than you lose, and to keep losses small when you do lose.*
